# Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Workforce and Capacity in Sub-Saharan Africa

## Overview

This repository supports a multi-country assessment of the plastic and reconstructive surgery (PRS) workforce, service delivery, and facility capacity across Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA).

The study uses a cross-sectional survey of accredited plastic and reconstructive surgeons to characterize workforce distribution and density, surgical practice and workload, facility resources, and training capacity across the region.

Data collection is planned for August–September 2026 using an electronic questionnaire administered through KoboToolbox.

## Research Questions

This study seeks to answer the following questions:

- How many plastic and reconstructive surgeons are currently practicing in Sub-Saharan Africa, and how are they geographically distributed?
- What types and volumes of plastic and reconstructive surgery procedures are performed by accredited plastic surgeons?
- In which facilities and sectors do accredited surgeons provide care?
- What cross-country differences exist in workforce density, procedure volume, service availability, and training capacity?

## Objectives

To assess the distribution, density, and capacity of the plastic and reconstructive surgery workforce and the facilities in which they practice across Sub-Saharan Africa through a multi-country survey of accredited plastic and reconstructive surgeons.

## Study Design

This study employs a cross-sectional, multi-country survey design to assess the plastic and reconstructive surgery workforce and facility capacity across Sub-Saharan Africa.

## Study Setting

The study will be conducted across countries in Sub-Saharan Africa, with particular focus on countries affiliated with:
- the College of Surgeons of East, Central and Southern Africa (COSECSA); and
- the West African College of Surgeons (WACS).

## Sampling Strategy

A purposive census sampling approach will be used.

The study aims to identify and invite all accessible accredited plastic and reconstructive surgeons currently practicing in Sub-Saharan Africa. Potential participants will be identified through sources including:
- COSECSA
- WACS
- National plastic surgery societies
- Hospitals
- Accredited surgical training programs
- Professional networks

Snowball sampling will supplement the initial sampling frame. Participants may identify additional eligible surgeons who were not captured through the initial databases and professional networks.

Because the study seeks to enumerate the accessible population of practicing accredited plastic surgeons rather than estimate population parameters from a representative sample, no formal sample size calculation is planned.

Recruitment will continue throughout the study period to maximize participation and improve the completeness of the regional workforce assessment.

## Data Collection
Identification of Participants

Potential participants will be identified through multiple professional and institutional sources.

For the COSECSA region, participant identification will include the COSECSA Capsule database, as described in previously published methodological work. Additional sources will include WACS databases where available, national plastic surgery societies, hospitals, accredited training programs, and professional networks.

The use of multiple sources is intended to improve coverage of the eligible study population and identify surgeons who may not be represented in a single database.

## Survey Domains

The survey will collect standardized information across the following domains:

1. Demographic and Professional Characteristics

Information describing the participating surgeon's demographic characteristics, qualifications, professional experience, and accreditation.

2. Practice Location and Facility Distribution

Information on the countries, geographic locations, facilities, and health sectors in which surgeons provide plastic and reconstructive surgical care.

3. Workforce Capacity

Information describing the availability and composition of the surgical and supporting workforce.

4. Surgical Scope and Workload

Information on the types and volumes of plastic and reconstructive surgical procedures performed.

5. Infrastructure

Information describing facility infrastructure relevant to the delivery of plastic and reconstructive surgery.

6. Equipment and Supplies

Information on the availability of essential equipment, materials, and supplies required to provide surgical care.

7. Training and Workforce Development

Information describing training programs, educational capacity, workforce development, and opportunities for expanding the PRS workforce.

## Data Management

Participant confidentiality and data security are central to the study.

All records will be anonymized using unique participant identifiers. Research data will be stored on secure, password-protected systems, with access restricted to authorized members of the research team.

Data management procedures will comply with applicable ethical requirements and data protection regulations.

Personally identifiable or otherwise sensitive participant-level data will not be included in the public repository.

## Data Analysis

Data will be cleaned, structured, and analyzed primarily using R.

Analyses will generate summaries at multiple levels:
- Surgeon level
- Facility level
- Country level
- Regional level
- Cross-country level

## Descriptive Analysis

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize participant characteristics and survey responses.

Country-level workforce density will be calculated as:

PRS workforce density = (Number of practicing PRS surgeons / Population) × 100,000

Regional and country-specific estimates will therefore be reported as the number of practicing plastic and reconstructive surgeons per 100,000 population.

Publicly available population estimates will be used as population denominators.

## Geographic Analysis

Geographic mapping will be used to visualize the distribution of plastic and reconstructive surgeons across Sub-Saharan Africa.

Where data permit, analyses may examine:
- Surgeon distribution by country
- Subnational geographic distribution
- Urban–rural distribution
- Facility distribution
- Geographic variation in workforce density
- Geographic variation in service availability

## Reproducibility
